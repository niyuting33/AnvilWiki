## What can the demo be judged on now?

The demo provides evidence about its pixel-art presentation, current combat feel, menu navigation, equipment feedback, and the appeal of the Vitromotus premise. It can also show whether the combination of dungeon action and town rebuilding interests a player. Those observations must remain attached to the demo build.

| Visual identity               | Directly observable in demo and official media |
| Basic combat feel             | Directly testable in demo                      |

## What should not receive a final score yet?

The complete story, launch roster, later areas, co-op, console releases, and version 1.0 systems are not available for a final verdict. A planned feature should not add points to a current score, and a missing future feature should not be reviewed as though it failed to install.

Use the [demo guide](/guides/demo/) to test the public build, the [gameplay overview](/guides/gameplay/) for confirmed systems, and the [Early Access page](/guides/early-access/) for the announced scope.

## How will this review be completed?

The launch review should record the tested build, PC hardware, settings, session length, and reproducible issues. It should assess combat readability, progression pacing, town utility, stability, controls, and whether the available content matches the store description.

## What are the demo's clearest strengths?

The strongest evidence is visual and structural. Emberville presents a detailed pixel-art world with a contrast between a ruined town and the dangerous Vitromotus, then connects that premise to action combat and rebuilding. That identity is visible without guessing final content totals.

The demo lets players judge movement, attack feedback, interface clarity, and whether directional combat suits them. Those impressions are personal but valid when tied to the tested build. They should not become a score for systems the preview does not contain.

Voice presentation and official media communicate character and tone. Their presence does not prove story length or later narrative quality. A responsible preview can call the presentation promising while leaving the complete story **[待补充]**.

## Which concerns deserve launch testing?

Controls need testing across keyboard, controller, and any supported handheld context. A navigation problem fixed in one demo patch may behave differently elsewhere. Performance also needs hardware, settings, resolution, scene, and duration before it becomes reproducible.

Progression depth cannot be judged from an opening sample. Classes, weapons, equipment, gathering, crafting, farming, cooking, residents, and town facilities need a longer build. Economy and balance are especially sensitive to patches.

The delay to January 7 followed demo feedback and more work on underlying systems, with bow combat cited as an example. That explains why judgments should wait; it does not prove every concern is fixed. Current launch notes and tests must provide that evidence.

## How should readers interpret preview coverage?

Previews describe a specific session and may use different builds, routes, hardware, and limits. Agreement is useful context, but it does not create verified health values, drops, recipes, or release features. This review synthesizes attributable facts rather than copying competitor wording.

When a detail cannot be traced or reproduced, it remains **[待补充]**. A confident tone is not a substitute for a source. Older footage stays useful only when its version and limits are clear.

## Who is the current demo likely to suit?

It is most relevant to players interested in pixel-art action RPGs, combat plus life skills, and town rebuilding. Players who require finished content, stable balance, co-op, or native consoles should wait. The [multiplayer](/guides/multiplayer/) and [console](/guides/consoles/) pages track those separately.

The free demo makes this a low-risk preference test on Windows PC. Use the [demo guide](/guides/demo/) for the official download and requirements. Do not use an unofficial build to form a review of the current game.

## What will a full review measure?

The final method will record build, hardware, settings, input, time played, and reached content. It will assess combat readability, class and weapon differentiation, progression pacing, resource usefulness, town utility, story delivery, stability, accessibility, and whether the store description matches the build.

Any numerical score, if used, must follow the evidence. Until the Early Access build is tested, this page remains `draft: true`. That prevents a preview from masquerading as a launch verdict.

## How will value be judged fairly?

Value will be compared with the live regional price and the content actually present in the tested build. A planned feature cannot justify the current price before it ships, while an unfinished system should be judged within the expectations clearly disclosed by Early Access. The [price page](/guides/price/) keeps commercial facts separate from review opinion.

Session length alone will not determine value. Repetition, meaningful progression, encounter variety, town utility, stability, and the desire to continue all matter. Exact completion time and endgame depth remain **[待补充]** until the broader build is available.

## How will updates affect the verdict?

The review date and build will appear with every major reassessment. A later patch can fix a control problem, change balance, add content, or introduce a regression. The page should describe that evolution rather than silently applying a launch score to every future version.

Version 1.0 will require a separate review decision because it represents a different release stage. Until then, conclusions must remain about Early Access, not the imagined finished game.
