## Why should you clear egg sacs first?

Preview observations describe egg sacs as an important source of pressure in the arena. Leaving them active while focusing only on the boss can allow smaller threats to accumulate and reduce your safe movement space. Clearing nearby sacs early simplifies the encounter and makes later boss attacks easier to read.

Do not cross the entire arena blindly to reach a sac. Move when the boss is not forcing an immediate response, keep a route away from surrounding enemies, and avoid committing to a long animation beside multiple threats. The objective is control, not destroying every target as fast as possible.

If the current build changes how sacs behave, follow what you can reproduce. Record whether a sac creates enemies, blocks movement, or simply acts as a target in that version. Exact spawn timing and sac durability remain **[待补充]**.

## How should you handle the smaller spiders?

Remove nearby smaller enemies before they surround you or obscure the boss's next action. They may be less dangerous individually, but several angles of pressure can interrupt healing and punish a long attack sequence. Keeping their number low preserves attention for the boss.

Use positioning that keeps enemies on a readable side of the character when possible. Running into an uncleared corner can trade one threat for several. If your weapon has a broad swing or ranged option, test its behavior rather than assuming it is always safer.

Target priority can change with distance and immediate danger. A spider already attacking may matter more than a distant sac, while a clear boss opening may be worth a short sequence if the adds are controlled. This guide recommends a priority framework, not a fixed scripted order.

## When is it safe to attack Spider Matriarch?

Attack after the boss has committed to a readable action and you have enough space to leave. Use short sequences until you know how quickly the boss recovers or turns. Greedy attacks are risky because the arena also asks you to monitor sacs and smaller enemies.

Watch the end of your own animation as carefully as the boss. If an attack prevents movement, count that commitment as part of the opening. A theoretical extra hit is not useful when it removes the time needed to dodge the response.

Repeat successful openings before expanding them. One safe sequence provides evidence; surviving a longer sequence once may simply mean the boss selected a different action. Exact attack timing and phase behavior remain **[待补充]** and should be tested per build.

## How should you use healing food?

Heal when you have created distance and can see the important threats. Trying to recover while a smaller spider is already attacking can waste the item or leave you vulnerable. Clear immediate pressure or move into an open lane first.

Do not wait for a specific health percentage copied from another guide. The correct moment depends on the recovery animation, incoming threat, and the food available in your build. Exact food values and recommended quantities are **[待补充]**.

If healing repeatedly fails, identify why before adding more food. The issue may be input selection, an interrupted animation, insufficient space, or misunderstanding how the current item works. A reproducible cause is more useful than treating the encounter as a pure inventory check.

## What should you do if the arena becomes crowded?

Stop attacking the boss and restore control. Move toward open space, clear the nearest smaller enemies, and deal with accessible sacs when the boss allows it. Continuing a damage race while surrounded usually makes the encounter less readable.

Avoid circling without looking at the path. Environmental edges, obstacles, sacs, and enemies can narrow the route. A deliberate reposition that keeps threats visible is safer than sprinting into an uncleared area.

If the build provides a way to reset the encounter, use it only after recording what went wrong. Did sacs remain active, did smaller enemies accumulate, did healing fail, or did a long animation trap you? The answer determines what to change on the next attempt.
